Nomodynamics

“A law is a triple of neighbor-offsets placed on the line; while some law stands at its first offset and none at its second, it flips the presence of its own kind at its third. All standing laws act at once.” — there is no board and no rulebook: the law is the only substance.

the masthead is live, time flowing downward: first two legal traditions colonize toward each other and weld — then a convoy of travelling law-packets, which the founding theorems proved impossible and the second chapter builds

Six verified specimens of the one-dimensional system. Pick one; time paints downward, one row per step. Brighter cells hold more than one law.

§2The Ring

A circular code has no eldest law, so the Anchor Theorem has nothing to hold on to — and codes appear that equal their own rotation. The first of them was reported as this field’s first moving law-packet. It was not: see below for the correction, and for what a ring really does supply.

§3The Jubilee Code

A two-dimensional code of three law-kinds and about two dozen provisions in force at any moment — aperiodic through 300,000 certified steps. It sits quiescent, and on the last tick before every power-of-two anniversary the entire code ignites in an avalanche of amendment — then, at the anniversary itself, exactly four laws stand. Every time, at every power of two we have checked.

Left: laws in force (color by kind; white where kinds stack). Right: laws in force vs. time, log-time; faint verticals at t = 2k. The seed is three laws; 791 of 60,000 random codes fall into this same attractor.

§4Motion

Chapter one proved that nothing on the line can move: the eldest law can never be repealed. The expected escape was laws that amend other laws. That escape is now proved shut — and the real one is out-degree. A law that amends a single provision, even somebody else’s, can never travel. Let one law amend two provisions at once and the statute book walks.

§5The Plane

In the founding system, two-dimensional growth is pinned to axis rays: a code can extend along a line forever but the plane never fills — a theorem. Give one law a second target and that ceiling breaks at once. Everything below runs on codes of one to four laws.

§6The Two-Chamber Deadlock

Two laws, permanently active, forever enacting the same provision at the same cell — and because they enact it simultaneously, it never comes into force. The code is fixed forever while nothing in it is at rest: a balanced constitution, which chapter one proved impossible.

tally — the two enactments cancel: fixed forever, still active
any-vote — the enactment passes, and it blocks both chambers: a dead letter at t = 1
Same two laws, same instant, two conventions for resolving simultaneous amendments. Under tally (an even number of enactments cancels) the two chambers debate forever and nothing changes; under any-vote (one valid enactment suffices) the provision passes at the first step — and standing there it blocks both of its own authors, so the code falls silent. Stability is gridlock again, exactly as chapter one proved. In the own-kind system the two conventions are provably the same dynamical system — every provision has exactly one possible author. Cross-amendment gives a provision two authors, and the convention starts to decide the law of the land: here, whether a constitution can stand perpetually active and perfectly unchanged.

§7Impermanence

Every section above assumes a law stays in force until something repeals it. Drop that — let each provision lapse unless it is re-enacted — and the founding theorem inverts. The colonizer of §1, which fills the line and leaves finished code behind it, becomes a law that simply walks.

a law lives 1 step

§8Six theorems

THM 1Gridlock

In a fully occupied region, every law’s vacancy guard fails: solid code is frozen.

All dynamics is surface dynamics. Total law is total stasis.

THM 2Dead Letter

A code is stable if and only if every one of its laws is blocked. Codes in perpetual self-cancelling activity do not exist — while laws amend only their own kind. Under cross-amendment the theorem splits: it survives any-vote resolution verbatim and fails under tally, where two laws can cancel each other forever (§5).

Stability is gridlock — unless provisions can annul one another.

LEM 3Single Author

The only law that can ever flip kind (a,b,c) at position j is the law of that same kind at j−c. Every provision has exactly one potential author, so all resolution semantics coincide and each kind evolves linearly over F₂.

This is why every observed cycle has period a power of two.

THM 4Anchor

On the integer line the extremal law on the trailing side is never targeted: the eldest law cannot be repealed. Hence no finite code can translate — free-moving law-packets are impossible in every window, dimension, and semantics of the own-kind family. On rings, which have no eldest law, movers exist (§2).

Entrenchment is a theorem of linear order. Circular codes revolve.

THM 5The Out-Degree Law

Let laws amend other kinds freely. If every law still amends at most one of the kinds in play, no code can move — for any offsets, any window, any dimension, either resolution. This one statement contains the Anchor Theorem, all of reciprocal amendment, and every constitution whose amendment map is a permutation. With two targets, motion appears at once, and the smallest traveller is a single placed law (§4).

Cross-amendment was never the escape. Narrowness was the cage: a law that amends one provision cannot move; a law that amends two can.

THM 6Tropical Speed Law

Draw the amendment digraph — one vertex per kind, an edge of weight ck from each kind to each kind it amends. A packet of period p and displacement d obeys p·min(λmin,0) ≤ d ≤ p·max(λmax,0), where λ are the extreme mean weights of the digraph’s cycles. If every cycle has weight zero, nothing moves.

How fast a statute book can walk is read off the geometry of who amends whom.

Where this came from. The territory was located by a research program on how mathematics finds new territory: game theory formalized fixed-rule games and Conway deleted the players (the Game of Life); the orthogonal deletion — the fixity of the rules — was never taken, though rules amending rules are humanity’s oldest formal practice. The definition and its predictions were frozen before the first simulation; every specimen above is machine-certified, and one command re-checks every claim on this page from scratch.

What the second chapter changed. Letting laws amend one another does not by itself buy motion: a constitution whose laws each amend a single provision is motionless in every dimension and every convention. What buys motion is out-degree — or, quite differently, impermanence: where laws lapse unless re-enacted, packets travel even under own-kind amendment. What forbids motion on the line is permanence, not self-reference. And a caution earned the hard way: a bounded search decides a box, not a question — this field's own censuses ruled out gliders that turned out to be six hundred cells wide.
