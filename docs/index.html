Nomodynamics

“A law is a triple of neighbor-offsets placed on the line; while some law stands at its first offset and none at its second, it flips the presence of its own kind at its third. All standing laws act at once.” — there is no board and no rulebook: the law is the only substance.

§2The Ring

On the infinite line, the Anchor Theorem holds: the eldest law can never be repealed, so no law-packet can travel. A circular code has no eldest law — and it revolves.

§3The Jubilee Code

A two-dimensional code of three law-kinds and about two dozen provisions in force at any moment — aperiodic through 300,000 certified steps. It sits quiescent, and at every power-of-two anniversary the entire code ignites in an avalanche of amendment and collapses back to a handful of laws.

Left: laws in force (color by kind; white where kinds stack). Right: laws in force vs. time, log-time; faint verticals at t = 2k. The seed is three laws; 791 of 60,000 random codes fall into this same attractor.

§4Four theorems from day one

THM 1Gridlock

In a fully occupied region, every law’s vacancy guard fails: solid code is frozen.

All dynamics is surface dynamics. Total law is total stasis.

THM 2Dead Letter

A code is stable if and only if every one of its laws is blocked. Codes in perpetual self-cancelling activity do not exist.

Stability is always gridlock; there are no living monuments.

LEM 3Single Author

The only law that can ever flip kind (a,b,c) at position j is the law of that same kind at j−c. Every provision has exactly one potential author, so all resolution semantics coincide and each kind evolves linearly over F₂.

This is why every observed cycle has period a power of two.

THM 4Anchor

On the integer line the extremal law on the trailing side is never targeted: the eldest law cannot be repealed. Hence no finite code can translate — free-moving law-packets are impossible in every window, dimension, and semantics of the own-kind family. On rings, which have no eldest law, movers exist (§2).

Entrenchment is a theorem of linear order. Circular codes revolve.

Where this came from. The territory was located by a research program on how mathematics finds new territory: game theory formalized fixed-rule games and Conway deleted the players (the Game of Life); the orthogonal deletion — the fixity of the rules — was never taken, though rules amending rules are humanity’s oldest formal practice. The definition and its predictions were frozen before the first simulation; every specimen above is machine-certified, and the no-go theorems were checked against 15.3 million certified runs. The door out of this solvable sector is cross-amendment — laws that amend other kinds — where the linearity provably breaks. That territory is open.
